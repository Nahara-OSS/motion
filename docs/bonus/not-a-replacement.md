## Introduction

The main selling points of Nahara's Motion to most people are that it is both
freeware and free software:

- **Freeware** means we distribute this software at no price, and so people can
  legally obtain this software without spending a single dime, which is ideal
  for people to get started with motion graphics without spending a fortune;

- **Free software** means you have the freedom to use, modify, share and
  redistribute the software (and its source code), and in special cases, you
  must retain the freedom of others when redistributing the software.

Most people only care about **freeware** part when it comes to Nahara's Motion,
which is totally expected as most creative softwares are weirdly expensive for
some reason. However, Nahara's Motion is **not meant to be a replacement** to
other existing tools, and so to think of Nahara's Motion as freeware alternative
is somewhat a wrong idea.

In reality, the purpose of Nahara's Motion is to just offer a tool that does the
motion graphics on its own way:

- Instead of having two separate objects and modifiers system, Nahara's Motion
  consolidates both systems into one through the use of render targets;

- Instead of storing the objects in a flat hierarchy, Nahara's Motion stores the
  objects in a tree hierarchy, where an object is a child of either the scene
  root or another object;

- Because of tree hierarchy, Nahara's Motion can use the variable system without
  introducing any kind of circular dependencies in the scene tree.

The points above already made Nahara's Motion completely different from other
motion graphics apps, thus making it not suitable to be used as drop-in
replacement. In other words, if you already have experience with other apps, it
is better to keep using those in your professional environment if you don't have
time to learn and explore, and time is important.

## Why would you _want_ to pick Nahara's Motion, exactly?

So... why would you want to use Nahara's Motion exactly?

- If you never tried motion graphics before, Nahara's Motion can be your
  starting point, because if you don't have experience, you essentially want to
  learn a new tool. We tried our best to make the newcomers' experience as good
  as possible, though it is not always possible to make the editor easy to learn
  while cramping features to make the editor somewhat capable. If you are new,
  we recommend checking out [Quick start guide](../editor/quick-start.md).

- If you found someone doing something that's complicated in other apps, but
  really simple or intuitive in Nahara's Motion, or there is something unique
  that only exists in Nahara's Motion then Nahara's Motion is the good choice
  for that. Getting started with [Quick start guide](../editor/quick-start.md)
  is still highly recommended, but you can also try to find your way in.

- If you think [the architecture](./architecture.md) of Nahara's Motion is good
  then Nahara's Motion is the perfect choice for you. In this case, it is best
  to explore the user manual in depth. The
  [editor's full introduction page](../editor/index.md) is a good place to get
  started.

- If you just want to do motion graphics for fun or as a hobby, you are not
  doing any serious business, and you can't access to your favorite motion
  graphics app anymore (either too expensive to use or there is a limited number
  of seats your employer currently have), you might want to use Nahara's Motion.

- In case your employer or team lead asked you to switch to Nahara's Motion
  (mostly to make things more manageable), you have to use Nahara's Motion,
  though it might be better if you ask them to reconsider again.

- There are more reasons that made you want to consider using Nahara's Motion.
  In any cases, you can refer to [Quick start guide](../editor/quick-start.md)
  to get started, or [full introduction page](../editor/index.md), depending on
  how much time you want to spend on exploring and how much knowledge you wish
  to learn.

## Why you _shouldn't_ consider Nahara's Motion?

We try to make Nahara's Motion easy to use while being capable as much as
possible, so that it can adopts to a wide demographic. That said, there are
cases where it might not be the good idea to consider using Nahara's Motion:

- If you are looking for free drop-in replacement to your favorite tool,
  Nahara's Motion might not be the most suitable for that purpose. When you want
  an alternative, it implies you want to do the way you've been doing on your
  favorite tool, but now with different tool that have lower price and/or more
  usable.

  The case with Nahara's Motion is that it does things _differently_ than other
  tools that doing the same thing. If you are using the old tool in your
  professional environment, it is best to keep using it since you already have
  experience with it than it is to learn how to use Nahara's Motion, unless
  there are other things that worth more than "just a free alternative", like
  the [free software philosophy][fsf] for example.

- If you are looking for so-called "industry standard" then unfortunately,
  Nahara's Motion might not be what you want. Although we try our best to use
  open standards, there are cases where the industry standard is proprietary and
  a lot of professionals just happens to use it, which we can't implement
  without _violating_ the laws.

- There are more little reasons where it's best _not_ to consider Nahara's
  Motion.

## Integrations

The true power of a tool should be about integrating it with existing workflow.
So basically, if you treat Nahara's Motion as just another tool in your toolbox,
you will gain a new power. There are things that do really well with other tools
but does badly in Nahara's Motion, but there are also other things that does
best in Nahara's Motion while being hard or impossible to do in other tools, and
if you know how to harness the power, you can greatly improve your workflow.

As such, it is more ideal to approach Nahara's Motion with the mindset of _"What
can I do with Nahara's Motion?"_. _"Can I do what I've been doing in X?"_ is a
wrong mindset because, again, not every tools are alike.
